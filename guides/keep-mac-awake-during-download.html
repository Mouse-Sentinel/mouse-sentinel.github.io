Keep a Mac awake during downloads and uploads

A sleeping Mac can pause some browser downloads, cloud uploads, backups, and file transfers. The reliable fix is to prevent idle system sleep for the duration of the job.

By Mouse Sentinel · Updated September 9, 2026 · 6 minute read

Quick answer

Connect your MacBook to power, keep its lid open, and temporarily prevent idle system sleep. You can change macOS settings, run caffeinate in Terminal, or use a keep-awake utility. A mouse mover is useful when you also want periodic cursor activity and an obvious visual countdown.

Why transfers sometimes stop

Display sleep and system sleep are different. A dark screen does not necessarily mean the Mac has stopped working, but full system sleep suspends most applications and network activity. Some apps resume cleanly after wake; others time out, lose a session, or require the transfer to be restarted.

Browser behavior, cloud clients, network drives, and backup tools differ. Start by checking whether the application has its own “prevent sleep” feature. For a critical transfer, verify that progress continues after the display turns off before walking away.

Option 1: change macOS settings temporarily

Open System Settings and review Lock Screen, Battery, and Energy Saver. On supported configurations, enable the option that prevents automatic sleeping on the power adapter when the display is off, and set the display timeout to suit the task. Labels vary by macOS release and hardware.

This approach needs no additional software, but remember to restore your normal energy settings afterward. Keeping a Mac awake uses more energy and may generate heat during demanding work.

Option 2: use caffeinate

Apple includes the caffeinate command with macOS. Open Terminal, enter caffeinate, and leave that Terminal process running during the transfer. End it with Control-C when the job is complete.

For a timed session, caffeinate -t 7200 requests two hours of sleep prevention. The timer is measured in seconds. This is convenient for technical users, but it does not move the pointer and the Terminal window is the main indicator that the session is active.

Option 3: use a visible keep-awake tool

A menu bar utility is easier when you do this regularly. Mouse Sentinel requests idle display and system sleep prevention while enabled and makes a small pointer movement after a configurable period without user input. Its countdown shows when the next movement will happen.

Before leaving a long transfer

  • Connect power for long or resource-intensive work.
  • Keep a MacBook lid open unless you are using a supported closed-display setup.
  • Confirm the transfer destination has enough space.
  • Check that VPN or cloud authentication will not expire.
  • Disable sleep only for as long as needed.
  • Follow device-management policies on work and school Macs.
Important: a mouse mover cannot guarantee that a particular website, cloud service, VPN, or remote server will keep a session alive. Those systems can impose their own timeouts.

Which method is best?

Use macOS settings for a simple occasional change, caffeinate for a precise command-line session, and a dedicated utility for repeatable one-click control. Choose a mouse mover only when pointer movement is part of the workflow; preventing sleep alone may be enough.
